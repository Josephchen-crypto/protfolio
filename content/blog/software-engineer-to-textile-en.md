I wrote code for over a decade. Then I switched into textile fabric trading. It's been roughly a month.

My last courier run took me to Yiwu — that was my first real exposure to textiles. I tagged along with my brother, watched him look at fabric, asked questions, got handed samples. I probably retained very little from that trip, but one thing landed: **this industry looks nothing like what I had in my head.**

Once I was back home, I started digging into the basics. I wanted to actually understand terms like fiber blend, width, motif scale, and weight — words I couldn't even use without sounding lost in conversation.

## 1688 Was My Bridge

Earlier this month my sister asked me to learn the 1688 platform — upload main images, enter product data, that kind of thing. For someone who used to be a programmer, it was almost too simple. It's all CRUD — image upload, data entry, batch-editing specs.

Mid-month I went around factories with my brother. We visited suppliers, drank tea, caught up on the latest products. After one round of shadowing, he sent me out on my own the next day.

## The Real Problem Wasn't Technical — It Was Talking to People

Eleven years of point-to-point: office and home. Apart from coworkers, I rarely talk to people. Especially strangers.

So I stood there feeling awkward. Just awkward small talk. Being a programmer doesn't transfer that easily, by the way. I had to use what I know about tech, plus the bits of textile business I've picked up, as the angle to talk to factory owners who've been in this industry for almost 20 years. They're interested in what I bring — I hope.

But awkward is awkward. That's just how it is.

## Right Now, I Don't Know the Industry at All

I'll be honest. I barely understand the details. Pricing negotiation, MOQ math, shipping lead times — I'm still at the surface.

So my plan is to run two tracks in parallel:

First, keep learning. Some concepts aren't clear yet, and I need a few more rounds of working through them with an AI tutor.

Second, I can't just wait until I've "finished learning" and then start. I have to move. I'm going public with this — through self-media — and aiming to land one real customer inquiry within a month.

## Self-Media: Remotion or Hyperframes

Speaking of self-media, my brother has been nudging me to pick up a video editing tool and put content out on platforms. Use the traffic to find customers.

I agree with the direction. I looked at a couple of technical approaches. Two stood out for me.

One is **Remotion** — a framework built with React.js, naturally a fit with Next.js. Write components, and code becomes video.

The other is **Hyperframes** — built on plain HTML + CSS + JS, the most basic web stack. Compared to Remotion, the upside is that AI agents work with it much more naturally. You can have AI help write the code, which speeds up production.

But I'm personally leaning toward Remotion. It's React — that's the front-end stack I've been working in for years. The learning curve is low, and I can pick it up immediately.

## Editing Software Is Not My Path for Now

There's also the option of using a video editor directly. But I don't know editing tools.

After some thought, I'm going with the harder technical route. It fits what I'm actually good at — I'm a software engineer, and the value I bring is doing things differently than everyone else. That's what makes it interesting.

## What I'm Doing This Week

Here's the one-week target list:

1. Lock down the foundational industry knowledge — well enough that I can hold a real conversation with factory staff, not just awkward small talk.
2. Pick Remotion or Hyperframes and ship one video episode as a learning project — figure out the front-end stack by doing, not by reading.
3. In parallel, keep working with AI on two projects: this blog (feature work and improvements) and an MVP product-matching platform I can use to find what a customer wants and route it to the right factory quickly.
4. Refresh my foreign-trade site — switch the overall look to something more like a professional textile supplier, not the portfolio it is today.

Hope it all goes smoothly.

A small addendum before I hit publish: I'll come back to this post in a month and see which of these targets I actually hit.
